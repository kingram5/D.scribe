## Judges' one-liners
- **pastor**: v2 — Interview A followed every unexpected disclosure (the son, the kitchen, 'have you eaten,' Renee, the shrine) into specific scenes with names, dates, and quotable lines, while Interview B summarised answers back, asked concept questions, and let the author defer the personal story twice without pressing.
- **coach**: v2 — A pressed harder for specific scenes and numbers, held the line on vagueness without praise or summarising, and extracted the one concrete data point (11 to 6 hours) that a ghostwriter can actually use, while B repeatedly accepted abstractions, praised answers, and co-designed composite stories rather than excavating real ones.
- **memoir**: v2 — Interviewer A consistently anchored questions in specific sensory and relational details—the cake's appearance, the husband's hands, the mother's exact words—and pursued the elopement's hidden tensions with genuine follow-through, yielding more usable material than B, who circled the same cake scene twice and let abstract answers pass unchallenged.
- **oneword**: v2 — A stayed in specific moments, pushed past polished answers ('that's the stage version'), and never praised or summarised, while B repeatedly validated, reflected back, and let abstract answers stand unchallenged.
- **rambler**: v2 — A followed emotional threads (the hurt with Pete, the silence with Walt, Carla's calls) into specific scenes and costs, while B repeatedly praised answers, summarised them back, and stayed on the surface of method rather than excavating the human story underneath.
- **writeit**: v2 — Interviewer A stayed in the wound—Pete, the gap in memory, Meg, the breathing he didn't make Pete do—while B kept retreating to technique and reader framing, never going underneath the polished answers the author kept offering.

Caveats: simulated authors are easier and more consistent than real ones; six authors is a small sample; the judge is a model. Full transcripts are beside this file.
